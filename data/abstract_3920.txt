The exponential growth in digital data generation has highlighted the density limits of traditional storage media, which are based on magnetic and electronic supports. As an alternative, synthetic DNA data storage emerges as a promising medium due to its high information density and the longevity of its molecular structure under appropriate preservation conditions. However, the sequencing process responsible for reading these data is characterized by significant noise rates, especially insertion, deletion, and substitution (IDS) errors, which compromise the synchronization of the stored sequences. To mitigate this problem, two distinct approaches have been employed: algebraic error correction codes, such as Reed-Solomon, originating from Computer Science; and Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA) algorithms, such as MAFFT, originally conceived within Bioinformatics for phylogenetic analysis and later adapted for error correction in DNA storage. This work proposes the development of an interactive simulator, built in Python and Streamlit, to compare the performance of Reed-Solomon and MAFFT under different error rates, maintaining a comparable sequencing depth between the two approaches. The aim is to identify the error rate at which the data recovery capability of one algorithm surpasses that of the other, contributing to filling a gap identified in the literature reviewed in this work: the absence of a direct comparison between the two methods under equivalent conditions.
